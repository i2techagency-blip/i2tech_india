What Should Your Website Design Quote Include?

A useful quotation explains what will be designed, what your team needs to supply and how the website will be handed over. Compare the scope as carefully as the price.

Pages, Content & Visual Design

  • List the pages and distinct layouts: home, services, products, about, contact and any industry-specific pages.
  • Agree who supplies copy, photography, logos and product information, and whether writing or image sourcing is included.
  • Define the design review stages and revision allowance before work begins.

Features & Mobile Journeys

  • Specify enquiry forms, editable content, product filters, booking tools or other features you actually need.
  • For ecommerce, discuss the catalogue, payment provider, delivery rules and checkout requirements.
  • Include responsive layouts and testing of the actions customers will take on a phone.

Ownership, Launch & Support

  • Confirm domain ownership, hosting arrangements, admin access and the agreed source-file handover.
  • Separate one-time design costs from hosting, licences and ongoing maintenance charges.
  • Agree launch checks, the support period and how future updates or additional work will be quoted.

What Changes the Cost?

Page count is only part of the estimate. Original layouts, content preparation, catalogue size, integrations and migration work can change the effort considerably. A focused business website and a large online store should not be compared as the same project.

Share your existing website, essential pages, required features, target launch date and budget range. We can then discuss a suitable scope and quotation.

How to Choose the Right Website Design Partner in Delhi

Five practical checks help separate a capable long-term partner from a template seller.

I
Strategy Before Screens

A credible agency should ask about your audience, positioning, competitors and conversion goals before discussing colours or layouts. Strategy gives the website a commercial purpose.

II
Portfolio and Proof

Review relevant work, live websites, independent feedback and the clarity of the agency's own digital presence. A strong portfolio should demonstrate both design range and practical execution.

III
Responsive Quality and Performance

Ask how mobile UX, accessibility, loading performance, semantic page structure and browser testing will be handled. These foundations should be considered during design, not treated as emergency repairs after launch.

IV
Clear Scope and Communication

Expect a written scope, milestones, responsibilities and review process. Clear communication protects the budget, keeps approvals moving and reduces avoidable revisions.

V
Support After Launch

Clarify maintenance, security, backups, analytics and future optimization before signing. A useful website keeps improving as customer behaviour, technology and search requirements change.
